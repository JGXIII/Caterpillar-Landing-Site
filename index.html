Log it your way

Log a meal in seconds, not minutes

Pick whichever suits the moment. Caterpillar learns your portions and habits as you go.

Snap Your Plate

Take a photo and Caterpillar picks out each food on the plate and estimates its calories and macros. On iPhone Pro models with LiDAR, it measures your actual portion too. Know it was 130 g of pasta? Say so, and it uses your number.

Barcode scans

Point, beep, logged, with strong coverage of Woolies, Coles and Aldi. Got a few packets to log? Log & Scan Next takes you straight to the next one.

Menu Scan

Eating out? Snap the menu and tap what you ordered, then size each dish up or down to match what landed on the table. Logging the pub lunch takes less time than ordering it.

Just say it

Describe what you ate the way you'd tell a mate, out loud or typed, with no ingredient-by-ingredient entry. "Maccas" and "two Weet-Bix with milk" land exactly where you'd expect.

Favourites that stick

The foods you have every day sit one tap away, saved at the portion you actually have. Shakes, powders and mixed drinks too.

Your recipes

Save your own, or share a recipe screenshot or web page straight into the app. Send one to a friend and it lands in their Caterpillar.

Numbers you can trust

Honest tracking, without the guesswork

Caterpillar is built around honest food tracking. From understanding cooked versus dry food weights to using Australian nutritional data, we want your numbers to reflect what you actually eat.

Cooked, not dry

Pasta, rice and oats roughly triple in weight when they're cooked, so 130 g dry and 130 g cooked are very different meals. Log "130 g pasta" over a plate of dinner and Caterpillar counts it as cooked, and says so on the entry.

Hydration, counted properly

A dew drop fills with everything you drink, coloured by what it was. Coffee and tea count: they hydrate you about as well as water. Spirits work the other way, so a neat nip takes a little water back out of the drop, and you can watch it happen.

Australian food, first

Built on FSANZ data, Australia's own food composition figures, so local food is counted with local numbers. Add barcodes, nutrition panels and a shared verified-food database that gets better every time somebody scans something new.
